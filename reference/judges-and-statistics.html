Judges and small counts

Verified 2026-11-10 · Vally 0.21.0

How to write a judge, how to measure it, how to give it a scale, how to judge two versions side by side, and how to read a count from a handful of trials. Both calculators from the lessons are on this page.

Writing a judge

  1. Use a judge only for what code cannot decide.
  2. Read real entries and write your own yes or no for each before the judge runs.
  3. Write one criterion, answerable yes or no.
  4. Name the judge model, and choose one that is not under test.
  5. Ask the judge to quote what it judged, so each verdict can be matched to a trial.
In a stimulus
    rubric:
      - The new changelog entry says what changes for people
        who use the tool, and does not say how it was coded.
    graders:
      - type: prompt
        config:
          scoring: binary
          prompt: Begin your reason by quoting the new entry.

The judge model is named once, as judge_model under defaults. A judge call that fails is not retried: the grader records status: error and the stimulus shows ERROR; fix the cause and re-grade the saved trials. Lesson 12, Lesson 20

Measuring a judge

NumberMeaningLow means
TPROf your yes labels, the share the judge passedFalse alarms: stricter than you
TNROf your no labels, the share the judge failedMissed failures: more lenient than you

Agreement alone hides which way the errors run: a judge that always says yes scores well whenever most labels are yes. The aim suggested by practitioners is above 90% on each number, on a hundred or more labels for a judge you depend on. Lesson 13

Label again when the rubric's wording changes, when the judge model changes, when the judge is pointed at a different kind of request, or when its scale or threshold changes. Keep some labels aside while rewording a rubric.

A judge on a scale

evals/wording-scaled.eval.yaml (one judge)
      - type: prompt
        config:
          scoring: scale_1_51
          threshold: 0.752
          prompt: Begin your reason by quoting the new entry.
  1. The judge gives each rubric criterion a whole number from 1 to 5. This is the default when scoring is left out; scale_1_10 also exists.
  2. The judge's own pass line, on the rescaled score. Left out, it is 0.5, so a 3 passes. Lesson 14

vally grade prints the judge's reason and its tick or cross, and the trial's Score line, but not the judge's own number. With one other grader that passed, read the answer off the Score line as above.

  • Use a scale when work can be partly right and you want to know how far off a failure is. Keep checks that must all hold out of any weighted score.
  • Two thresholds, two jobs. The judge's threshold decides its tick or cross. A top-level scoring block's threshold decides the trial, from the weighted scores, and then the judge's tick no longer matters.
  • In our run on twelve labelled entries, the 1-to-5 judge at 0.75 changed one verdict, passing an entry we had failed: TNR fell from 6 of 6 to 5 of 6. At the default 0.5, TNR would have been 3 of 6.
  • Judged again, three of twelve answers moved by one point, and the entry at the threshold lost its pass. Scores near the threshold are where run-to-run variation changes verdicts.
  • With weights of 0.5 each and a threshold of 0.75, two entries that failed the judge passed as trials. That is a failure hidden in an average.

Reading a small count

A count is an estimate with a range around it. These ranges are our arithmetic, using the Wilson interval at 95%.

Judging two versions side by side

vally compare pairs the baseline's and the treatment's saved trials by stimulus and trial number, and a pairwise judge says which answer of each pair is better. It reads each pair twice, in opposite order, because judges tend to favour one position. Lesson 19

  • --fail-on-regression exits 1 only when the mean is below zero and its whole range is too. It ignores the metric deltas and McNemar. In our terra comparison tokens rose by 11,132 a trial, range +665 to +21,599, and it exited 0.
  • McNemar on one-way flips alone: 5 give p = 0.063, and it takes 6 to go below 0.05 (p = 0.031, our arithmetic). The pairwise judge can be surer, because it also credits a pair both versions failed but one failed less badly.
  • Our nine pairs were three stimuli tried three times, not nine independent requests, so the range is narrower than the evidence allows. Trust the sign and whether the range crosses zero, not the second decimal: swapping the two roles gave −0.49, not −0.60.
  • A pairwise judge is a model too, and we did not measure it against labels as in lesson 13.

What makes a result defensible

  • The same stimuli, graders and models on both sides, run in one sitting.
  • Counts reported, per model, with the number of trials.
  • Ranges that do not overlap, or several small results that agree.
  • A mechanism you can see in a patch, and where possible a control, such as the run with no skill.
  • For "is it worse?", a side-by-side comparison whose range lies wholly on one side of zero, with the metric lines read as well.

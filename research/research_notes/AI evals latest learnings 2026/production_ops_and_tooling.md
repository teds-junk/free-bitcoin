# Operating evals in production and across teams: online evaluation, observability, CI, and tooling (state as of 2026-11-09)

Scope note: this builds on `/Users/ted/src/learn/vally/research/eval-best-practices-digest.md` (compiled 2026-09-10) and does not repeat it. Items marked **[new since digest]** are either published after that digest or not covered by it. Every URL below returned HTTP 200 on 2026-11-09 unless flagged. Source-type tags: **[primary]** = the organisation's own engineering/research/docs page; **[vendor]** = a company describing its own product or a survey it ran; **[practitioner]** = an individual's blog; **[secondary]** = a summary of someone else's material.

## 1. How do mature teams connect offline evals, CI gates and online/production evaluation? What is the "eval flywheel" and what evidence exists that it works?

### Takeaway
The flywheel is now described almost identically by every vendor and practitioner (observe production traces, analyse failures, turn them into offline eval cases, improve, repeat, with the same scorers reused online), and the 2026 additions are an explicit third stage: A/B or "online experiments" that calibrate judges against user outcomes. Evidence that it works is still mostly case-study and vendor-survey grade; the strongest independent data points are Spotify's eval-to-experiment calibration argument, Datadog's worked experiment, and the Nova Escola case, not controlled studies.

### Cited Findings
**The flywheel as currently described**
- Braintrust (27 Mar 2026) defines four stages: observe ("logging every input, output, trace, and failure"), analyze, evaluate ("every production failure is a candidate for your eval suite"), improve; and four maturity stages: 0 vibes, 1 on-demand test sets, 2 "automated and integrated into CI/CD", 3 "production data continuously flows back into your eval suite". **[vendor]** — [Braintrust, "Evals are the new PRD"](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- The same post lists implicit production signals to mine: "A thumbs-down can become a new eval case. An edited output can serve as ground truth. A copy-paste followed by task completion can indicate success. A person rephrasing the same prompt three times may reveal an eval gap." **[vendor]** — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- Phil Hetzel (Braintrust, AI Engineer Europe 2026) frames it as "evals build confidence before deployment; observability maintains it afterward", and says the dataset should "include production traces—or at least traces from user acceptance testing ... Think of evaluation as rerunning production". The talk's maturity framework is four overlapping areas (getting started, measuring to manage, accounting for complexity, advanced techniques), explicitly "not rigid stages". **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer, "The maturity phases of running evals"](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- Origin story for online evals from the same speaker: an early Braintrust customer was "running a massive evaluation every hour of every day against production traffic" by piping production traffic into an offline-eval product, which is what pushed the vendor into tracing; "The same scoring functions can also run against observability traffic as online evals, supporting alerts". **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer, "Why building eval platforms is hard"](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/_fQ7Z_Wfouk-why-building-eval-platforms-is-hard)
- Hamel Husain's version (Pragmatic Engineer, 2 Dec 2025) is "Analyze → measure → Improve → automate → start again", with "Use evals in the CI/CD pipeline, but use production data to continuously validate that they work as expected, too." Claimed base: "over 40 other companies" and "over 3,000 engineers" taught. **[practitioner, foundational for this period]** — [The Pragmatic Engineer, "A pragmatic guide to LLM evals for devs"](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals)
- Microsoft Foundry (Build 2026, 3 Jun 2026) ships the same loop as named features, all Public Preview at Build: "Evaluations with intelligent trace sampling" ("Evaluating every production trace is wasteful; evaluating none is risky"), "Traces to dataset" ("Production traces are the highest-fidelity test data you have"), and "Trace replay and visualization"; it describes "the closed observe → evaluate → optimize → deploy loop". Tracing and evaluations themselves "reached general availability" in spring 2026. **[vendor] [new since digest]** — [Microsoft Foundry blog, Build 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/build-2026-from-observability-to-roi-for-ai-agents-on-any-framework/)

**Online evaluation mechanics (sampling, judges, experiments)**
- Sampling rates actually reported: Nova Escola runs "a suite of evals daily against 2% of production traffic" (~1M monthly users, 200,000 teachers). **[practitioner case note]** — [Hamel Husain, "Case Study: Putting Evals Into Production"](https://hamel.dev/notes/llm/ai-product-engineering/evals-production.html). Datadog caps automated routing of traces into human annotation queues at "up to 5% ... for example, 2% of matching traces". **[vendor docs]** — [Datadog annotation queues](https://docs.datadoghq.com/llm_observability/evaluations/annotation_queues/)
- Spotify (18 May 2026) positions evals and A/B tests as "a funnel, not a fork": "evals belong before your experiment, not instead of it"; "Evals verify: does the output conform to quality standards? Experiments validate: do real users respond as predicted?" Numbers: "only about 12% of A/B tests end in a shipped positive result", "Around 64% produce valid learning", and "teams roll back about 42% of launched experiments to prevent regression in secondary metrics ... No evals or offline evaluation flagged those." **[primary engineering blog] [new since digest]** — [Spotify Engineering, "Better Experiments with LLM Evals"](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/5/better-experiments-with-llm-evals-a-funnel-not-a-fork)
- Spotify's feedback-loop addition: "run your LLM evals on the A/B test data itself. Did the version the judge preferred actually perform better with users? ... When the gap between eval scores and experiment outcomes is large, that's diagnostic gold." And the warning: "Without offline-online signal calibration, our evals are opinions, not evidence." **[primary]** — [Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/5/better-experiments-with-llm-evals-a-funnel-not-a-fork)
- Datadog (4 Nov 2026) launched "Online Experiments": random assignment of live traffic between agent variants with online-evaluator scores as experiment metrics and confidence intervals. Its own worked example (model swap in its Insights agent, 50/50 split): estimated cost "44.3% lower" (CI 37.5% to 51.2% lower); judge quality "3.84 ... and 3.86 ... although close averages alone do not establish equivalent quality"; duration best estimate +12.8% (CI 4% lower to 30% higher); workflow errors +13.6% (CI 16% lower to 43% higher). Trace review then found the new model "supplied invalid tool parameters more often", which the 1-5 judge score had not surfaced. **[vendor, but reports numbers and a negative finding] [new since digest]** — [Datadog, "Compare AI agent changes with Online Experiments"](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/online-experiments-agent-observability/)
- Lyft's customer-support agent pipeline (2026 talk, via summary) has an offline gate (simulated multi-turn conversations plus LLM judges) and then "an online evaluation pipeline [that] continuously monitors performance through tracing tools, online graders, and human-in-the-loop error analysis that feeds insights back to development teams". Offline data is built by "sampling actual production conversations ... and mutating different criteria" rather than prompting an LLM for test queries. **[secondary summary of a talk; original at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3z2uT5aDx_Y not reviewed]** — [ZenML LLMOps database, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)

**Adoption data**
- LangChain "State of Agent Engineering" (n=1,340; fielded 18 Nov-2 Dec 2025): 89% have some observability, 62% detailed tracing; 52.4% run offline evals on test sets; 37.3% run online evals (44.8% among those with agents in production); "not evaluating" is 29.5% overall and 22.8% among those in production; "Of the organizations running any evals, nearly a quarter combine both offline and online"; human review 59.8%, LLM-as-judge 53.3%; quality is the top production barrier (32%). **[vendor survey; respondents skew to the vendor's audience]** — [LangChain, State of Agent Engineering](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)
- Inngest "AI in Production" (n=130, 5 May 2026): "35% of respondents building AI in production are not doing AI evals at all. Of the 65% who are, most have built their own pipeline"; growth-phase teams (11-50 engineers) are highest at 44% not doing evals. Cited gaps: hard-to-write evals 31%, LLM-as-judge cost 25%, "results in a separate system (20%), no way to act on a failed eval (13%), evals offline and disconnected from production (10%)". **[vendor survey, small n]** — [Inngest, AI in Production 2026](https://www.inngest.com/blog/ai-in-production-report-2026)
- Amplify Partners 2026 AI Engineering Report: "there's no widespread agreement on what the biggest challenge is. Evals, orchestration and agent logic, inference, and security are all within a few points of each other"; "the majority of respondents are still (just) updating their prompts" when adapting behaviour based on evals; "Almost half of respondents are actively monitoring cost in prod". **[investor survey]** — [Amplify Partners, The 2026 AI Engineering Report](https://www.amplifypartners.com/blog-posts/the-2026-ai-engineering-report)

**Evidence that the flywheel works**
- Inngest reports correlation, not causation: "The strongest statistically significant predictors [of team confidence in scaling] are production evals and the ability to diagnose failures fast—both clearing p<0.05 independently"; "the absence of evals is the clearest anti-pattern in the dataset (p=0.038)". Outcome variable is self-reported confidence, n=130, and the surveyor sells an adjacent product. **[vendor survey]** — [Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/blog/ai-in-production-report-2026)
- Case-level evidence: NurtureBoss found via error analysis that "just three issues accounted for most problems: date handling, handoff failures, and conversation flow issues". **[practitioner]** — [The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals). Datadog's experiment above caught a tool-parameter regression before rollout. — [Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/online-experiments-agent-observability/)
- Counter-evidence that offline evals alone mislead: Spotify cites a case where a vendor's "coding evals showed no improvement" for a model release that "had improved substantially on longer tasks a controlled experiment would have surfaced. Miscalibration runs both ways." — [Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/5/better-experiments-with-llm-evals-a-funnel-not-a-fork)

### Inferences
- The 2026 shift is from a two-box picture (offline evals, production monitoring) to a three-stage funnel: offline evals screen candidates, online evaluators score sampled live traffic, and randomised experiments both validate business impact and recalibrate the judges. Spotify and Datadog independently describe the same structure within six months of each other.
- Online judge scores are being treated as experiment metrics with confidence intervals, which imports experimentation discipline (random assignment, guardrail metrics, intervals) into eval practice. The Datadog example shows why an aggregate 1-5 judge score is not enough on its own: the regression was visible only in traces.
- Survey numbers agree on rough shape (about half of teams run offline evals, about a third run online evals, a quarter to a third run none) but come from vendor audiences and are not comparable in detail.
- The "flywheel compounds" claim is asserted far more often than it is measured. No source found quantifies quality improvement per flywheel cycle.

### Gaps
- No controlled or longitudinal study found showing that teams running the flywheel ship measurably better products; all outcome evidence is case study, self-report, or vendor survey.
- I searched for 2026 first-party posts on production eval operations from Shopify, Notion, GitHub, LinkedIn, Stripe and Honeycomb and found none in search results; absence from search is not proof they do not exist.
- The Lyft material is a third-party summary of a conference talk; figures and names in it (for example "UserAlen", probably a transcription error for a Microsoft user-simulation paper) should be checked against the recording.
- The LangChain page displays "12 June, 2026" but describes a survey fielded in Nov-Dec 2025; treat the data as end-of-2025.
- Amplify report publication date and respondent count were not captured from the page (a search summary said 1,048 respondents).

## 2. Current practice for running agent evals in CI: tiering, budgets, flakiness, caching/replay, gating

### Takeaway
Practice has converged on a tiered design (smoke subset per PR, core suite on merge/nightly, adversarial "torture" suite daily or weekly), relative-to-baseline gating rather than absolute thresholds, and new metrics introduced in observe-only mode before they block. The genuinely new 2026 material is about noise that is not the model: Anthropic showed infrastructure configuration alone moves agentic coding scores by up to 6 points, and deterministic replay of tool and external state is acknowledged as unsolved rather than a solved caching problem.

### Cited Findings
**Infrastructure noise and flakiness [new since digest]**
- Anthropic (5 Feb 2026): "infrastructure configuration alone can produce differences that exceed those margins. In internal experiments, the gap between the most- and least-resourced setups on Terminal-Bench 2.0 was 6 percentage points (p < 0.01)." **[primary]** — [Anthropic, "Quantifying infrastructure noise in agentic coding evals"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)
- Infra error rates went "from 5.8% at strict enforcement to 0.5% when uncapped"; "as many as 6% of tasks were failing because of pod errors". From 1x to 3x resource headroom "success scores fluctuate within the margins of noise (p=0.40)"; above 3x, extra resources change what the eval measures. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)
- Recommendation: "evals specify both parameters per task" (guaranteed allocation and a separate hard kill threshold), with the band "calibrated so that scores at the floor and ceiling fall within noise of each other"; a 3x ceiling cut infra errors "5.8% to 2.1%, p < 0.001". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)
- On SWE-bench (227 problems, 10 samples each) scores "were only 1.54 percentage points higher at 5x than 1x" RAM. "We have observed anecdotally ... that pass rates fluctuate with time of day, likely because API latency varies with traffic patterns and incidents." Rule of thumb: "leaderboard differences below 3 percentage points deserve skepticism until the eval configuration is documented and matched"; "Naive binomial confidence intervals already span 1-2 percentage points; the infrastructure confounders ... stack on top of that". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)
- Tooling response: Arize Phoenix's Harbor plugin (8 Sep 2026) records an `infra_ok` evaluation per trial that "helps distinguish low task scores from execution errors". **[vendor docs]** — [Arize Phoenix release notes](https://arize.com/docs/phoenix/release-notes/09-2026/09-08-2026-harbor-plugin-for-agent-benchmarks)
- Practitioner framing: "Flaky evals are not flaky tests ... An LLM eval that fails intermittently might be signal — the model is near the threshold ... Don't treat eval variance as flakiness to suppress." **[practitioner]** — [Tian Pan, 17 Apr 2026](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)

**Tiering and budgets**
- One practitioner's tiers: "30–80 tasks total", split into "Smoke (10 tasks): <2 minutes, runs on every PR", "Core (30 tasks): runs on merges and nightly", "Torture (10–20 tasks): adversarial, slow, runs daily/weekly"; tasks tagged with step, tool-call and latency budgets; "every golden suite should include at least 5 tasks that are designed to fail" to prove the harness detects bad behaviour. **[practitioner, 5 Aug 2026]** — [Kunal Ganglani, "Agent Evaluation Harness"](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/agent-evaluation-harness-replay)
- Another: "Run the full suite on scheduled nightly builds and on merges to main; run a stratified sample of fifty on every PR. The stratified sample should include your hardest cases". Pipeline order: lint/type-check, then a 10-20 input smoke test, then a stratified sample with a budget ceiling compared with the production baseline. Claimed cost "around $0.15–0.30 per merge for a mid-sized production LLM feature" (author's estimate, no data shown). **[practitioner]** — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Cost controls from the same post: tiered judges (cheap model screens, expensive judge only on flagged subset); "Every LLM API call in CI should route through a layer that enforces a per-run cost ceiling. If a CI step hits the ceiling, it fails the step with a cost-overrun error". — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Deterministic budget checks as scorers: Braintrust's talk shows separate 0/1 scores for tool-call budget and token budget, because "an agent that exceeds a tool-call budget or consumes too many tokens can be checked deterministically". **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals). The practitioner version: "CI gates for agents must cover step budget, tool error rate, latency, cost caps, and safety constraints, not just 'accuracy.'" — [Kunal Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/agent-evaluation-harness-replay)
- LLM-judge cost is a named barrier: 25% of Inngest respondents cite it. **[vendor survey]** — [Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/blog/ai-in-production-report-2026)

**Gating policy**
- "A comparison baseline, not an absolute threshold ... run the eval on both the candidate version and the current production version, compute the delta, and fail the build if the new version scores statistically worse". **[practitioner]** — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- "Soft blocks over hard blocks for new signals ... run it in observation mode for a few weeks before making it a hard gate ... Rushing a new metric straight to a hard gate often results in the team disabling it after the first false positive." And "Gate on what you can measure ... format compliance, refusal rate on adversarial inputs, factual accuracy on questions with known ground truth, response latency distribution. Abstract qualities belong in periodic human review cycles". — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Lyft's small-sample warning: 84% vs 88% "based on only 50 examples ... is too small to be meaningful"; rigour is reserved "for moments when numbers actually gate decisions or inform leadership". **[secondary]** — [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- Braintrust's PRD mapping puts "Automated eval gates in CI/CD" as the replacement for a launch-readiness review and "Regression thresholds that define minimum quality bars" for roadmap priority. **[vendor]** — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)

**Caching and replay**
- Inspect AI local cache keys: model name and base URL, prompt (message history), epoch number, generate config, active tools and tool_choice; default expiry 1 week; pitched for "increased determinism" when iterating on a scorer. Provider-level caching is separate. **[primary docs]** — [Inspect AI, Caching](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/caching.html)
- Practitioner cache key: "a hash of (prompt_version, model_id, input)". — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates). Practitioner replay claim: "Replay (recorded tool outputs + sandboxed side effects) is the difference between stable, cheap agent testing and flaky theater"; keep a live mode for canaries. — [Kunal Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/agent-evaluation-harness-replay)
- Replay is not solved for stateful agents: Hetzel names two problems, reconstructing external-system state as of the original request and not overwriting production data on create/update/delete, and "describes this as an incompletely solved problem". Options he lists: mock APIs, storing relevant system state inside the trace and injecting it at eval time, or time-travel queries against stores that keep history. **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- The mocking counter-argument: "Mocking those dependencies gives you a clean green build and a false sense of security ... when you mock perfect responses, you only test the happy path." — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Vendors are productising replay for debugging rather than CI: Foundry "Trace replay and visualization" (Public Preview) with the tip to "replay the exact trace that broke and re-run it against the candidate fix". **[vendor]** — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/build-2026-from-observability-to-roi-for-ai-agents-on-any-framework/)

### Inferences
- Agent-eval variance now has three separable sources that need different handling: model sampling (multiple trials, in the prior digest), judge noise (calibration, in the prior digest), and infrastructure (resource headroom, time of day, sandbox provider). Only the third is new, and Anthropic's numbers imply a CI regression gate tighter than about 2-3 points on a sandboxed agentic suite is gating on noise unless the infra configuration is pinned.
- An `infra_ok`-style signal recorded per trial is the practical way to keep infra failures out of the pass-rate denominator; retrying only infra-failed trials is defensible, retrying model failures is not.
- Caching (same prompt in, same completion out) and replay (same world state) are being conflated in practitioner writing. Caching is a solved, tool-supported cost lever for scorer iteration; replay of a multi-step agent against mutable external systems is not, and the cache key stops matching as soon as the agent's first action diverges.
- Tier sizes and cost-per-merge figures in circulation are individual practitioners' numbers. They are consistent with each other but none is backed by published data from a named team.

### Gaps
- No first-party engineering post found giving a real team's CI eval cost, wall-clock, or flake rate over time. The "$8/PR" and "$0.15–0.30 per merge" figures that circulate are single-author estimates.
- No source found describing a principled retry policy (how many reruns before a failing agent task blocks a PR) with data.
- I did not verify how Harbor itself handles caching, retries or resource limits per task; only its trajectory format page was read.
- Whether per-PR path-scoped eval selection ("run only the evals touched by changed files") works in practice is asserted in blogs seen only as search summaries; not verified.

## 3. State of tracing standards and observability for agents, and why it matters for evals

### Takeaway
OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions are the de facto wire format but are still not stable: they remain in "Development", gained agent, evaluation-event and MCP conventions across v1.37-v1.41, and in mid-2026 moved to their own repository. In parallel, the benchmark world standardised on ATIF, a JSON trajectory format from the Harbor/Terminal-Bench project, and bridges between the two (ATIF trajectories rendered as OTel-style traces) appeared in September 2026. This matters for evals because traces are now the unit that offline datasets, online judges, annotation queues and experiments all consume, and a wave of vendor acquisitions made portability a practical concern.

### Cited Findings
**OpenTelemetry GenAI**
- The opentelemetry.io GenAI page now reads: "GenAI semantic conventions have moved to the OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions repository. This page has moved and is no longer maintained in this repository." **[primary] [new since digest]** — [OpenTelemetry docs](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/gen-ai/)
- The new repository covers "spans, metrics, and events for GenAI clients, MCP (Model Context Protocol), and provider-specific conventions (OpenAI, etc.)" and uses Weaver to depend on core semconv; its README still shows "Schema URL: TODO". **[primary]** — [open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai)
- Version history as summarised by Greptime (21 May 2026): v1.37 chat-history revamp (`gen_ai.system_instructions`, `gen_ai.input.messages`, `gen_ai.output.messages`; `gen_ai.system` replaced by `gen_ai.provider.name`); v1.38 "Evaluation event; tool definitions and call details; invoke_agent kind guidance"; v1.39 "MCP semantic conventions"; v1.40 retrieval span and cache-token attributes; v1.41 "execute_tool span naming requires tool name; reasoning tokens; invoke_workflow; streaming metrics; split invoke_agent into CLIENT/INTERNAL". **[vendor engineering blog summarising the spec]** — [Greptime](https://www.greptime.com/blogs/2026-05-09-opentelemetry-genai-semantic-conventions)
- Status: "As of May 2026, the GenAI and MCP semantic conventions remain in Development status ... No public timeline for stabilization", with `OTEL_SEMCONV_STABILITY_OPT_IN` provided to manage transitions. The GenAI SIG was formed April 2024. — [Greptime](https://www.greptime.com/blogs/2026-05-09-opentelemetry-genai-semantic-conventions)
- Vendor ingest: a 18 Oct 2026 directory built from vendors' own pages found "Thirteen of the twenty tools ... state OTel or OTLP ingest", including Langfuse, LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize Phoenix, W&B Weave, Datadog ("OTel 1.37+ GenAI conventions or OpenInference"), Honeycomb, Pydantic Logfire, Opik, Laminar and HoneyHive. **[secondary, quoting vendor pages]** — [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/llm-observability-acquisitions-2026)
- Microsoft Foundry: "Foundry's production-grade tracing and evals now extend to agents built on LangChain, LangGraph, OpenAI SDK, Microsoft Agent Framework, and any custom framework via OpenTelemetry" (Public Preview at Build 2026); "If your agents already emit OpenTelemetry spans, you're most of the way there." **[vendor]** — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/build-2026-from-observability-to-roi-for-ai-agents-on-any-framework/)

**ATIF and benchmark trajectories [new since digest]**
- ATIF "records an agent's complete interaction history as JSON: messages, reasoning, tool calls, observations, and metrics." Motivation: "Without a shared format, every viewer, dataset, and training pipeline needs an agent-specific parser." **[primary docs]** — [Harbor, ATIF](https://harborframework.com/docs/trajectory-format)
- "Harbor's current format is ATIF-v1.8"; fields are `agent`, `steps`, `session_id`, `trajectory_id`, `final_metrics` ("aggregate token, cost, and step metrics"), `subagent_trajectories`, `extra`. "Version 1.8 adds audio content parts"; "Version 1.7 adds embedded subagent trajectories, per-document trajectory IDs, llm_call_count". Harbor accepts v1.0 through v1.8. The spec lives as an RFC in the Harbor repo. — [Harbor, ATIF](https://harborframework.com/docs/trajectory-format); [ATIF RFC](https://github.com/harbor-framework/harbor/blob/main/rfcs/0001-trajectory-format.md)
- Harbor uses `trajectory.json` both to render runs in its viewer and to "seed a new session" in agents that support loading; "Producing ATIF and loading ATIF are separate capabilities." — [Harbor, ATIF](https://harborframework.com/docs/trajectory-format)
- Bridge to observability: Phoenix's Harbor plugin (8 Sep 2026) lands a Harbor job as "a dataset, one experiment per resolved agent configuration, a run per trial, and every verifier reward as a named evaluation", and converts the saved ATIF trajectory into "one trace and one session, with AGENT, LLM, and TOOL spans under a harbor.trial root", including `compaction` spans. "Tracing never changes a score" and "The sandbox stays offline." **[vendor docs]** — [Arize Phoenix release notes](https://arize.com/docs/phoenix/release-notes/09-2026/09-08-2026-harbor-plugin-for-agent-benchmarks)

**Transcript analysis as an eval layer [new since digest]**
- UK AISI and Meridian Labs released Inspect Scout, "a tool for conducting systematic, quantitative transcript analysis ... scaled to analyse thousands of transcripts in parallel" using "scanners" (examples: refusal, evaluation-awareness, command-error). The seven-step pipeline includes "manually inspect at least a few samples" and "Validate your scanner" against ground truth. **[primary, 25 Feb 2026]** — [AISI, "A pipeline for transcript analysis using Inspect Scout"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/a-pipeline-for-transcript-analysis-using-inspect-scout); [Inspect Scout docs](https://meridianlabs-ai.github.io/inspect_scout/)
- NIST's AI standards centre co-authored the underlying paper and says transcript analysis tools helped it "find and fix examples of cheating from our evaluations". **[primary, 18 Feb 2026]** — [NIST, "Analyzing Transcripts from AI Agent Evaluations"](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/caisi-research-blog/analyzing-transcripts-ai-agent-evaluations)
- Transect (7 Oct 2026), built on Scout, turns a long agentic transcript into one interactive report placing "activity labels, token use and recorded events on a shared timeline"; AISI cautions that LLM judges' "judgements can be wrong or misleading. Reviewers therefore need to inspect both the supporting transcript passages, and how the automated analysis was produced." **[primary]** — [AISI, Transect](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/transect-making-large-scale-agentic-evaluations-easier-to-understand)

**Why it matters for evals**
- Trace-level evaluation: "The unit of inspection expands from the output to the entire agent trace ... target evaluations at individual tool or MCP calls where necessary." **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- Portability: "the part of your stack that should not belong to anyone is the instrumentation and the eval suite"; advice is to instrument once with OTel, keep a collector you control, and "Keep evals in your repository. Test cases, scorers and thresholds belong in version control next to the code they test." **[secondary]** — [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/llm-observability-acquisitions-2026)
- Integration gap felt by teams: "Nearly half of all AI teams (47%) cite at least one of" results-in-a-separate-system, no way to act on a failed eval, or evals disconnected from production. **[vendor survey]** — [Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/blog/ai-in-production-report-2026)
- Storage is a real constraint on trace-based evals: Hetzel warns about very large traces as single database rows and says customers such as Notion drove demand for "full-text search across millions of traces". **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/_fQ7Z_Wfouk-why-building-eval-platforms-is-hard)

### Inferences
- Two families of trajectory format are settling in without merging: span trees over OTLP for production (OTel GenAI, plus OpenInference as a still-used alternative) and a flat step list in JSON for benchmark runs (ATIF). They carry overlapping content (messages, tool calls, token and cost metrics), and the September 2026 Phoenix plugin shows conversion is tractable, so an eval harness can emit ATIF and still land in an OTel-native trace store.
- "Development" status after two and a half years means attribute names can still change; anyone building scorers that read `gen_ai.*` attributes should pin a semconv version. The v1.37 message-attribute revamp is the kind of breaking change to expect.
- An evaluation event exists in the OTel conventions since v1.38, which is the first standard place to attach scores to traces; I did not confirm how widely it is emitted.
- Reusing a trajectory to seed a new agent session (ATIF loading) is a building block for replay-based evals, though Harbor frames it as session continuation, not as a determinism guarantee.

## 4. How multi-turn conversations and user simulation are evaluated now

### Takeaway
Simulated-user evaluation is mainstream (τ-bench lineage in research; "user simulation" and "multi-turn evaluation" shipping as platform features), but 2026 produced the first direct human-comparison evidence that LLM-simulated users are miscalibrated proxies: results swing by up to 9 points with the choice of user model, and simulators are too cooperative. The practical responses seen are constraining or fine-tuning the simulator, adding personas, grading on end state plus semantic judges, and versioning the simulator as part of the benchmark.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence on simulator reliability [new since digest]**
- "Lost in Simulation" (Seshadri et al., arXiv 23 Jan 2026, rev. 28 Jan 2026), a user study across the US, India, Kenya and Nigeria on τ-bench retail: "user simulation lacks robustness, with agent success rates varying up to 9 percentage points across different user LLMs"; "evaluations using simulated users exhibit systematic miscalibration, underestimating agent performance on challenging tasks and overestimating it on moderately difficult ones." **[peer-review status unclear, see Gaps]** — [arXiv:2601.17087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17087)
- Details from the paper body: about 40 participants per age × dialect/country group; with US participants agents reach a 45.2% success rate with a human-vs-simulated expected calibration error of 15.1; on the hardest task bin humans succeed 30.8% of the time while simulation underestimates. Agents succeed 50.6% with Standard American English participants vs 39.4% with AAVE participants (ECE 11.7 vs 20.3), and the dialect gap widens with age (about 19 points in the 55+ group). — [arXiv:2601.17087v2 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17087v2)
- Lyft's production experience matches: frontier LLMs "role-playing as customers, behaved too cooperatively and patiently", giving "artificially inflated performance metrics with pass rates around 90 percent"; fine-tuning the simulator "on real Lyft user verbatim ... caused evaluation scores to decrease", which the team treats as improved validity. They added personas such as "bypass users who immediately demand human escalation", "refund seekers, and AI skeptics". **[secondary summary of a talk]** — [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)

**τ-bench lineage [new since digest]**
- τ²-bench (ICML 2026 proceedings, PMLR v306) adds "a novel Telecom dual-control domain modeled as a Dec-POMDP, where both agent and user make use of tools", a compositional task generator, and "a reliable user simulator tightly coupled with the environment, whose behavior is constrained by tools and observable states, improving simulation fidelity"; it reports "significant performance drops when agents shift from no-user to dual-control". **[peer-reviewed]** — [PMLR, τ²-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/barres26a.html); [arXiv:2506.07982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982)
- The same repository has since shipped τ³-bench (v1.0: "voice full-duplex evaluation, the banking_knowledge domain and 75+ task fixes"), v1.0.1 (15 Jul 2026: "fixed banking_knowledge grading; results from before 1.0.1 are not comparable on that domain"), and τ-bench 2.0 (τ-i18n: six languages, text and full-duplex voice, a new `intake` domain with over 200 tasks, and post-hoc judges that "do not change the task reward"). **[primary repo]** — [sierra-research/tau2-bench](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench); task-fix paper [arXiv:2512.07850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850); voice paper [arXiv:2603.13686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686)
- 2.0 scores "are not comparable with the 1.x leaderboard". Listed differences include: "The text user simulator gets a persona block"; "Communication checks use a semantic LLM judge instead of substring matching"; the NL-assertion judge model changed; voice user-simulator default model changed; 50 reviewed task replacements each in English retail and telecom. — [sierra-research/tau2-bench](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench)

**Platform features and practice**
- Microsoft Foundry (Build 2026, Public Preview): "User simulation automatically produces multi-turn conversations and edge-case scenarios"; "Multi-turn evaluation scores agent quality across full multi-step conversations — capturing context carryover, reasoning consistency, and end-to-end task success", motivated by failure modes that "only show up when context accumulates: tone drift, lost goals, contradictions, safety regressions across long conversations." **[vendor]** — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/build-2026-from-observability-to-roi-for-ai-agents-on-any-framework/)
- Lyft's offline gate has three parts: "a representative synthetic dataset, an LLM playing the role of the user to generate complete multi-turn simulated conversations, and a grader such as an LLM-as-judge"; judges are binary and task-specific (for example an "education rubric" that fails the agent for over-educating when escalation is appropriate or escalating too soon). **[secondary]** — [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- Braintrust lists "simulated user interactions" only as a forward-looking item on a slide, not as an established practice. **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- Query ambiguity as a multi-turn eval confound: Husain argues many "model failures" are product failures to collect context, and that "The trace should preserve both the original request and the clarified intent" so evaluators can judge whether the agent acted reasonably on what it had. **[vendor write-up of a practitioner talk, 30 Jul 2026]** — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/rise-of-the-ai-engineer-why-ai-evals-fail-before-the-evaluation-begins/)

### Inferences
- The user simulator is part of the measuring instrument. Changing its model or prompt changes scores by amounts comparable to differences between agents (9 points in one study; τ-bench declares a whole leaderboard non-comparable after adding a persona block and changing judges). Simulator model, prompt and version should be pinned and reported like any other eval configuration.
- Miscalibration is not uniform optimism: simulators overestimate on moderate tasks and underestimate on the hardest ones, and proxy some user populations worse than others. A single "simulated pass rate" therefore cannot be corrected with one scaling factor; validation against real conversations has to be stratified by difficulty and user segment.
- Three mitigation patterns recur: constrain the simulator with tools and observable state (τ²), fine-tune or persona-condition it on real user text (Lyft, τ-bench 2.0), and seed scenarios from sampled production conversations rather than LLM-invented ones (Lyft). None removes the need for a periodic human-comparison check.
- Grading in the τ lineage is drifting from pure end-state comparison toward end state plus LLM judges for communication and speech quality, with the judges kept out of the task reward. That keeps the headline score deterministic while adding diagnostic judges.

### Gaps
- Venue of "Lost in Simulation" is ambiguous: search results showed both ICLR 2026 virtual-site listings and an ACL Anthology 2026 preview entry; arXiv lists it only as a preprint. Not resolved.
- Reported simulator error rates for τ-bench (a secondary source gave 40% total / 12% critical for original retail and 16% / 6% for τ² telecom) were not verified against the paper.
- A CMU "User-Sim Index" paper (humans 92.9 vs best LLM simulator 76.0 on a faithfulness score) surfaced only in a search summary of a paper-list page; unverified, not used above.
- No independent evaluation found of the vendor "user simulation" features (Foundry and others) against real users.
- No first-party product-team write-up found on evaluating long multi-session conversations with memory.

## 6. Organisational practices that help eval adoption stick

### Takeaway
The practices with the most support are: a domain expert defines "good" while engineers build the workflow; error analysis precedes rubric writing; reviewers record a written reason with each pass/fail; a small opinionated group designs evals; and there is a standing review cadence over production traces. Documented case studies with outcomes are few (Nova Escola, NurtureBoss, Lyft) and most of the cadence advice is practitioner opinion.

### Cited Findings
**Ownership and roles**
- "Product and AI engineers shape behavior; systems engineers make the agent run; subject-matter experts judge whether its responses make sense in the domain ... An eval platform must support that collaboration rather than assume engineers can evaluate the agent in isolation." Spreadsheets limit participation: "Nontechnical contributors may have the strongest domain expertise or the closest contact with users". **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/_fQ7Z_Wfouk-why-building-eval-platforms-is-hard)
- PM ownership framing: "The PM defines what 'good' looks like through structured, repeatable tests ... the PM sets the bar in evals, and the team iterates"; PM job is to "define what 'good' looks like in code, curate the data that reveals what 'bad' looks like, own the flywheel". **[vendor]** — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- Keep the design group small: "involving too many stakeholders in eval design. More voices lead to compromise-driven, unfocused evals, so keep the design team small and opinionated." — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- Rubric sign-off: "The rubric is a product decision, not an engineering one — get the team that defines 'done' to sign off on it." **[practitioner]** — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Lyft: "By collaborating closely with domain experts, the team developed metrics aligned with specific business goals"; annotation queues give experts "an easy-to-understand UI" instead of raw JSON. **[secondary]** — [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- Product before evals: Husain reports "eval reviews all the time where it's really obvious that the product is not built correctly ... Stop. This is a product issue", and useful evals "begin with product design, close inspection of real traces, and direct participation from domain experts". **[vendor write-up of practitioner talk]** — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/rise-of-the-ai-engineer-why-ai-evals-fail-before-the-evaluation-begins/)

**Rituals and cadence**
- A proposed PM weekly loop: "Monday. Review production traces. Flag 20 responses that missed the mark. Tuesday. Curate those 20 into 5 new eval cases ... Wednesday. Run the full eval suite against last week's model and this week's candidate. Thursday. Review the delta. Ship or don't." **[vendor, prescriptive not observed]** — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- A practitioner recommends a light weekly review of 10-20 outlier traces with a larger analysis every 2-4 weeks, and a single domain expert with final say. **[practitioner; seen via search summary only]** — [Tian Pan, "Eval Engineering for Production LLM Systems"](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-02-13-eval-engineering-for-production-llm-systems)
- Starting ritual: run about ten inputs, and for each collect "A thumbs-up or thumbs-down judgment" and "A written explanation of that judgment ... The explanation is the reusable part of the expert's knowledge." **[vendor talk]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- Incident ritual: "Wire trace replay into your incident review process." **[vendor]** — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/build-2026-from-observability-to-roi-for-ai-agents-on-any-framework/)
- Conventions as code: treat "the sampling policy the way you treat the eval rubric itself: versioned, code-reviewed, documented, with a changelog." **[practitioner]** — [Tian Pan, annotation queues](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-06-02-the-annotation-queue-your-humans-quietly-stopped-reading)

**Case studies with outcomes**
- Nova Escola (lesson planner; ~1M monthly users): two reported mistakes. First, "He wrote a rubric before doing error analysis. Since most of his criteria never appeared as failures, his team spent time labeling data they did not need." Second, "He built an eval for a problem that needed a simple fix" (a prompt change stopped it). Outcome: regressions caught "by running a suite of evals daily against 2% of production traffic". **[practitioner note on a talk] [new since digest]** — [Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/notes/llm/ai-product-engineering/evals-production.html)
- NurtureBoss (AI leasing assistant): moved from "looked good to me" shipping to trace review with a custom viewer; the founder, as domain expert, labelled handoff traces pass/fail with critiques; three failure modes dominated. **[practitioner, 2 Dec 2025]** — [The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals)
- Lyft (about two years of work): replaced generic framework metrics, where "A score of 0.5 on response helpfulness provides no clear guidance", with task-specific binary judges validated on about 100 hand-labelled examples split train/dev/validation, reporting precision and recall per judge. **[secondary]** — [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- Spotify: positions LLM evals as an extension of an existing experimentation culture ("Spotify already has a strong evaluation culture in the shape of experimentation. LLM evals extend that culture upstream"). **[primary]** — [Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/5/better-experiments-with-llm-evals-a-funnel-not-a-fork)

**Why adoption fails**
- "Teams also tend to run evals only at launch and then stop"; and Goodhart: "If you optimize purely for the metric, you'll game it. Keep tying eval scores back to real outcomes like task completion, satisfaction, and retention." **[vendor]** — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- A blocking gate that fires falsely gets switched off (see section 2, "the team disabling it after the first false positive"). — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-ai-agents-ci-pipeline-deployment-gates)
- Growth-stage teams defer: 44% of teams with 11-50 engineers building AI in production do no evals. **[vendor survey]** — [Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/blog/ai-in-production-report-2026)

### Inferences
- The independent cases agree on sequencing more than on tooling: look at traces first, involve the domain expert in defining pass/fail, validate judges on a small labelled set, then automate. Both Nova Escola mistakes are sequencing errors, and both are cheap to avoid.
- Organisations with an existing experimentation or ML-validation culture (Spotify, Lyft) adopt evals by attaching them to that culture; the hardest adoption case is the 11-50 engineer product team with neither.
- "Who writes evals" is shifting toward PMs and domain experts supplying judgments and criteria while engineers and coding agents write the scorer code. This is asserted mainly by vendors; the case studies show domain experts labelling, not authoring evals.
- A recurring rule for making gates stick: introduce as non-blocking, promote once the false-positive rate is known.

### Gaps
- No case study found that reports adoption outcomes quantitatively (for example, defect escape rate or time-to-ship before and after), or that follows a team for more than one release cycle.
- No first-hand description found of trace-reading sessions as a team ritual (who attends, duration, artefacts) from a named company in 2026.
- No source found on written eval conventions or contribution guides inside product teams (naming, directory layout, review checklists), beyond the general "keep evals in the repo" advice.
- The weekly cadence figures come from one vendor post and one practitioner blog; neither is an observed practice at a named team.

## 7. What is new in human evaluation and annotation practice for product teams

### Takeaway
Annotation queues have become a standard product feature fed by sampling rules, and the new thinking is about the queue as a sampler: human review capacity is fixed, so which traces reach a human, and how that is recorded, determines what the "human-graded" number means. Agreement is being checked more often, with one documented case of two annotators agreeing below chance until the rubric was rewritten with experts; the single-arbiter ("benevolent dictator") pattern persists but is now paired with explicit judge-vs-human validation.

### Cited Findings
**Queues, sampling and throughput [new since digest]**
- Datadog annotation queues are positioned for "identifying failure modes, validat[ing] automated evaluations, and build[ing] golden datasets"; each label can carry pass/fail "Assessment criteria" and a reasoning field; automation rules route traces by filter with a "sampling rate (up to 5% for annotation queues ...)"; guidance: "When multiple annotators work on the same queue, establish shared understanding of criteria" and "Use free-form notes to document why you applied specific labels." **[vendor docs]** — [Datadog annotation queues](https://docs.datadoghq.com/llm_observability/evaluations/annotation_queues/)
- Throughput ceiling (one practitioner's estimate): "A trained reviewer can carefully grade somewhere between thirty and a hundred traces per hour"; three part-time reviewers at four hours a week "tops out around twelve hundred careful grades a week". **[practitioner, 2 Jun 2026; estimates, not measured]** — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-06-02-the-annotation-queue-your-humans-quietly-stopped-reading)
- "The queue order is the silent sampler nobody chose"; if unsorted by design, the human-graded score becomes "a measurement of the ordering function". Four strategies with different purposes: random (unbiased aggregate), stratified (segment regressions), priority (low judge scores, user reports, judge disagreement), active-learning (least-confident cases); an example split is 40/40/20. — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-06-02-the-annotation-queue-your-humans-quietly-stopped-reading)
- Report coverage with the score: "the score, the sample size it was computed on, and the share of total traffic that sample represents", and distinguish sampled-ungraded, sampled-graded and never-sampled traces. — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-06-02-the-annotation-queue-your-humans-quietly-stopped-reading)
- Humans grade the grader: "the LLM judge is a compression layer in front of the annotation queue, not a substitute for it ... The human work shifts from 'grade traces' to 'grade the grader'"; track judge-human agreement on a held-out sample ("Cohen's kappa is a reasonable starting point") and "Re-sample monthly." — [Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-06-02-the-annotation-queue-your-humans-quietly-stopped-reading)

**Agreement and rubric quality**
- Nova Escola: "Lucas used two annotators, and they agreed less often than chance. The team had not defined what good looked like clearly enough, so they rewrote the rubric with their pedagogical experts and relabeled the data." **[practitioner note] [new since digest]** — [Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/notes/llm/ai-product-engineering/evals-production.html)
- AISI's transcript-analysis guidance on when one annotator suffices: objective questions are "easily checkable by a single human annotator"; subjective ones "may need multiple annotators to account for disagreements or biases." **[primary, 25 Feb 2026]** — [AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/a-pipeline-for-transcript-analysis-using-inspect-scout)
- Criteria drift restated in 2026 practice: "You don't really know what you want until you see it in action ... You can only specify a certain amount up front" (Husain); Lyft: criteria "should co-develop with the model as teams test evaluators and calculate precision and recall." — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/rise-of-the-ai-engineer-why-ai-evals-fail-before-the-evaluation-begins/); [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- Binary labels with reasons remain the default: NurtureBoss's expert gave "a binary PASS/FAIL score and, crucially, a detailed critique"; Lyft found "Binary classifications are significantly easier to calibrate"; a mental-health startup's 1-5 "helpfulness" and "factuality" dashboard was "unactionable". — [The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals); [ZenML, Lyft](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-grade-evaluation-systems-for-customer-support-ai-agents)
- But not universal: Datadog's own internal judge scores investigations "from 1 to 5", and the team notes "close averages alone do not establish equivalent quality". — [Datadog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/online-experiments-agent-observability/)

**Single arbiter vs multiple annotators**
- The single-expert pattern in practice: at NurtureBoss "The domain expert, NurtureBoss's founder, Jacob, reviewed traces ... and made judgment calls." — [The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals). A vendor makes the adjacent point about eval design: "keep the design team small and opinionated". — [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/evals-are-the-new-prd)
- Who should not be the sole arbiter: an Arize write-up of Husain's talk argues engineers can build the labelling workflow but should not be the only people deciding what good looks like. **[seen via search summary of this page]** — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/rise-of-the-ai-engineer-why-ai-evals-fail-before-the-evaluation-begins/)

**Tools and agent assistance [new since digest]**
- Error Discovery skill (Shankar; Hamel's note dated June 2026 per search): "A human reviewer annotates in a local review app while an AI agent updates the failure-mode taxonomy and picks the next samples", using "active learning to choose the next examples based on what you have already labeled" and checking "earlier records for each new pattern". Stated rationale: error analysis "is the most human-intensive part of evals". **[practitioner]** — [Hamel Husain, "Automating Error Analysis"](https://hamel.dev/notes/llm/ai-product-engineering/evals-error-analysis.html)
- Custom review surfaces are now a platform feature as well as a DIY pattern: Braintrust "custom annotation views that users can generate and tailor" (React), because "A generic trace dump may not match how an expert understands the work"; NurtureBoss "vibe cod[ed] a simple, custom data viewer" in "a few hours". — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals); [The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals)
- Automated failure discovery over production traces: Braintrust lists "Topic modeling across production traces" as an emerging pattern and has since shipped a "Topics" feature that classifies traces "by task, sentiment and issues" to build datasets and review queues. **[vendor]** — [AI Engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/talks/FB-MLPhL9Ms-maturity-phases-running-evals)
- Research-side review tooling: Transect gives reviewers a timeline of a long agent run with links back to transcript passages, on the principle that reviewers must "inspect both the supporting transcript passages, and how the automated analysis was produced." **[primary, 7 Oct 2026]** — [AISI, Transect](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/transect-making-large-scale-agentic-evaluations-easier-to-understand)
- Adoption level: among teams running evals, human review (59.8%) is still more common than LLM-as-judge (53.3%). **[vendor survey, end-2025 data]** — [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)

### Inferences
- The "benevolent dictator" pattern and inter-annotator agreement are complementary rather than opposed in current practice: one expert sets the standard, and a quick two-annotator agreement check is used as a rubric test. Below-chance agreement at Nova Escola was treated as a rubric defect, not an annotator defect.
- Human labelling effort is being redirected from grading outputs to three narrower jobs: discovering failure modes (with agents proposing taxonomy updates and next samples), producing the 100 or so labels that validate each judge, and auditing judges on a deliberately sampled slice.
- Sampling policy for human review is emerging as a first-class, versioned artefact. A human-graded score without its sample size, sampling strategy and traffic share is hard to interpret, and a change in sampling policy breaks comparability the same way a rubric change does.
- AI-assisted annotation (agent-maintained taxonomies, topic modelling) lowers the cost of error analysis but adds a new thing to validate; AISI's caution about checking LLM-produced labels against the transcript applies equally to product teams.

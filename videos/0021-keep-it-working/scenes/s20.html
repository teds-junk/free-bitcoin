1# Conventions for the changelog-entry evals
2
3## What earns a grader
4A failure we have seen in a real trial. One grader per failure 
↪mode, named after it.
5
6## Code first, judge second
7A judge only for what code cannot decide, with one yes-or-no 
↪criterion.
8
9## Where files live
10`evals/` beside `skills/`, never inside a skill. Grading 
↪scripts in `evals/grading`, brought in through the grading 
↪environment.
11
12## Before trusting a grader
13The oracle, with and without the golden patch. A grader that 
↪checks for absence must also fail a saved trial known to be 
↪bad. A new budget is re-graded against saved trials and 
↪must pass all the good ones.
14
15## Before trusting a judge
16Our own labels first. TPR and TNR reported with the number of 
↪labels. Label again when the rubric or the judge model 
↪changes.
17
18## What we run and when
19The quick suite with `--require-pass` after every edit to the 
↪skill. The full suite before sharing and when a model 
↪changes. A before-and-after experiment and `vally compare 
↪--fail-on-regression` before relying on an edit. 
↪With-and-without after a large rewrite.
20
21## How we report a result
22Counts, per model, with cost per passing trial. 
↪"These trials cannot tell" when the ranges overlap.
23
24## What a run may cost
25Budgets at about twice the largest typical trial: 215000 
↪tokens, 10 turns, 30 seconds, and a 1-minute limit on the 
↪agent. Re-tested for free whenever the skill, the model or 
↪the effort changes. Cost per passing trial compared before 
↪switching models, with the judge's calls counted on top.
26
27## When a run goes wrong
28The four questions in order: is the eval valid, did it 
↪complete, why was the result unexpected, does the fix work. 
↪Read what the run saved before running anything again; an 
↪ERROR is re-run or left out and said so, never counted as a 
↪failure of the skill.
